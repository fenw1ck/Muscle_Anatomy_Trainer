How This Works

Drag the picture to move it; zoom in with ＋ or by holding the left mouse button. Peel the muscles away layer by layer to reach the bones. Tap a muscle to see where it attaches, or tap a bone to see which muscles attach to it. Pick a level to choose which muscles to study: Core, Standard, Advanced or Expert. Each level includes the ones before it. The skeleton is a simplified schematic of 26 bone groups (an adult has 206 bones; ribs, vertebrae, hand and foot bones are grouped).

Moving around. Mouse: scroll or right-click to peel a layer away (scroll up to put it back); hold the left button to zoom in, hold the right button to zoom out; drag to move. Keys: + and − zoom, arrows move, 0 whole body, [ and ] change layer, T turns the figure around. Touch: pinch to zoom. Double-tap empty background to see the whole body again.

The frame under the picture. Turn swaps between the front and back drawings. − and ＋ zoom. Muscles or + Bones chooses what to draw, and Layer peels the figure inward. Fibres shows hatch lines along each muscle’s fibres with an arrow for which way to glide in a massage. Danger zones shows the areas that need extra care. ◀ ▶ step through body regions.

Modes. Learn: tap anything to see where it attaches. Practice and Speed run: quizzes. Meditate: a guided tour of one region. Massage Trainer: step-by-step routines with the muscles, directions and cautions marked.

Learn mode helpers. Choosing a muscle zooms to the part of the body it lives in. Press Help me find it to hide everything except the flashing muscle and the bones it attaches to. The ◀ ▶ arrows on the picture step through the muscles of the chosen group, shallow to deep and from the middle outwards; choose Whole body in the list to step through all of them. Flashcards shows one flashing muscle at a time (ordered or random) and reveals the name when you ask. Double-click a close-up box to enlarge it and study just that area. When you scroll to peek at other layers, the picture snaps back to the flashing muscle's own layer after a moment.

How you can help

Some muscle names are hard to say. If you know how one sounds in everyday English, pick it here, type it the way it sounds and press Suggest it.
